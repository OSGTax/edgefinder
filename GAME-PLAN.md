# Grass Stain League — Game Plan

*Working title. The repo will be renamed later.*

## The idea

A neighborhood baseball league played in people's backyards, in the spirit of the backyard baseball games we grew up with, but entirely original. The twist that makes it ours: **every kid is a mini adult.** They're 8 to 12 years old, but they dress, talk and play like very specific grown-ups: a CPA with reading glasses on a chain ("That run is non-deductible."), a retired plumber with a stick-on walrus mustache, a monster-truck announcer who can't speak below full volume, a deli-counter lady who takes everyone's order between innings.

Even the announcers are kids playing at being a network broadcast booth: Chet Valentine (age 10, clip-on tie) does play-by-play and Dottie Fairweather (age 12, "former big leaguer" from one season of tee-ball) does color.

**Everything is made in code.** There are no drawings, photos, 3D model files, sound files or music files anywhere in the project. Every kid, yard, uniform, logo, sound effect and song is generated by the program.

## Where it stands today: the 3D demo

You asked for a playable demo that shows what the final game should look and feel like before we build out more teams. That's what's here:

- **Two teams, one heavily detailed backyard.** The Maple Street Mudcats visit the Cedar Lane Comets at **Pool Party Paradise**, the Mendozas' place: a two-story house behind home plate with a back patio and string lights, where Mr. Mendoza (Hawaiian shirt, walrus mustache, spatula) works a smoking grill and turns to watch the big plays; an in-ground pool in shallow right field with a diving board, ladder, loungers and a flamingo floatie; a short white picket fence in right; a clipped hedge in left; big leafy trees that sway in the wind; flower beds, lawn flamingos, a garden gnome, a garden hose, a kid's bike in the grass; each team's "dugout" (a bench, a picnic blanket and a hand-painted sign). Around it is a whole neighborhood: houses next door, a street with parked cars, telephone poles, a swing set and trampoline in the neighbors' yards, and a water tower on the hill.
- **18 kids in real 3D.** Each one has a real skeleton and moves: batting stance and swing, windup and delivery, fielding crouch, running, throwing, diving, leaping catches, feet-first slides, cheering, sitting on the bench. They have big expressive faces with eyes that blink and follow the ball, and eight expressions (focused at the plate, yelling on a dive, sad after striking out). Each wears a team uniform with their name and number, plus their grown-up costume (mustaches, shades, ties, aprons, hats, a sponge beard...). Bench kids hold their persona props: a coffee mug, a clipboard, a briefcase, a microphone.
- **Seven traits per kid: Contact, Power, Speed, Fielding, Arm, Pitching, Control**, different for everyone. Contact is how often you hit it, Power is how far it goes; Fielding is the glove and Arm is the throw; Pitching is how fast and nasty the pitches are and Control is whether they go where you aim. Big kids like Bo and Hank crush the ball but whiff more; Priya and Ruby almost never strike out. **Everyone can pitch**, and a tired pitcher swaps positions with a fresh arm. Nine per team, no benches, no injuries.
- **The full game loop.** Pick your team, innings (3 or 6) and difficulty, then play: bat (aim and time your swing, Power and Bunt options), pitch (pick a pitch and where to put it), field (choose where to throw), run the bases (send runners or call them back). Specials and the Hype meter, splash doubles in the pool, home runs over the picket fence, announcers, popups, sound and music.
- **A TV-style camera.** A broadcast view behind the plate for each pitch, a high follow-cam for balls in play, a chase-cam that rides home runs over the fence, and slow flyovers between innings. Crushed balls get a split second of slow motion; a radar gun shows every pitch's speed; the catcher tosses the ball back after each pitch and batters drop their bats when they take off.
- **Menus.** The title screen floats over a live CPU game in the backyard. There's also a team picker, "Meet the Kids" (all 18 with 3D portraits, traits, bios and catchphrases), How to Play and Settings (sound, music, announcer voice, aim assist and graphics quality).
- **Three graphics levels.** "Beautiful" adds real 3D grass blades and sharper shadows, and "Fast" is for older phones. The game picks one automatically.
- **Latest polish pass.** The kids got a full art pass: real cap bills, hairlines, hair tucked under hats, eyes with catchlights that blink properly, mouths with teeth, glasses with lenses, fingers, and sleeves and pants that bend cleanly. The yard now loads roughly twice as fast and draws about a third less work each frame. The loading screen shows a progress bar and what's being built ("Filling the pool…", "Rounding up the Comets…").

**How we know it works:** automated tests play dozens of complete games at superhuman speed and check that the stats look like a real kids' league: right now about a .335 team batting average, around four runs and a home run or so per team per game, a few errors, strikeouts around 11% and walks around 2–3%. Another test plays as a pretend human with realistic reaction times, to make sure hitting is learnable and that difficulty actually changes things.

**What changed since the v0.1 prototype:** the flat drawn-in-code look is gone, replaced by real 3D. The pick-up draft and season mode were removed, as you asked. Seasons come back once there are 10 teams. The other 54 kids and 7 yards from the prototype are saved in the project history (commit `a00d3e8`) for when we build out the league.

## How it's built (in plain terms)

- **One game, three places.** The game is a web app, so it runs in any modern browser. For the app stores it gets wrapped in a native shell (a free tool called Capacitor) that turns the same game into an iPhone app and an Android app. One codebase, so fixes and new features land everywhere at once.
- **3D in the browser** uses Three.js, a widely used free 3D engine. The baseball simulation underneath is our own and runs separately from the graphics, which is why the automated tests can play thousands of games without drawing anything.
- **No ongoing costs to run it.** It's single-player with no servers. Settings stay on your device. Hosting the web version is free or nearly free.
- **App store costs:** Apple charges $99/year for a developer account, Google $25 once.

## Staying original (important)

We're making a game *inspired by* the feel of 90s backyard baseball games, not a copy. Every character, name, team, yard, logo, song and joke here is new. We don't use any names, characters, art or music from those games, and no real MLB teams or players. That keeps us clear for the app stores.
