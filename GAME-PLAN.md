# Grass Stain League — Game Plan

*Working title. The repo will be renamed later.*

## The idea

A neighborhood baseball league played in people's backyards, in the spirit of the backyard baseball games we grew up with, but entirely original. The twist that makes it ours: **every kid is a mini adult.** They're 8 to 12 years old, but they dress, talk and play like very specific grown-ups: a CPA with reading glasses on a chain ("That run is non-deductible."), a retired plumber with a stick-on walrus mustache, a monster-truck announcer who can't speak below full volume, a deli-counter lady who takes everyone's order between innings.

Even the announcers are kids playing at being a network broadcast booth: Chet Valentine (age 10, clip-on tie) does play-by-play and Dottie Fairweather (age 12, "former big leaguer" from one season of tee-ball) does color.

**Everything is made in code.** There are no drawings, photos, 3D model files, sound files or music files anywhere in the project. Every kid, yard, uniform, logo, sound effect and song is generated by the program.

## Where it stands today: the 3D demo, revamped for phones

**Play it:** https://osgtax.github.io/edgefinder/ (any modern browser, phone or computer; turn the phone sideways). On a phone you can install it: Android offers "Install app"; on iPhone use Share → "Add to Home Screen". Installed, it opens full screen, sideways, and plays offline. I refresh this link whenever the demo changes.

You asked for a playable demo that shows what the final game should look and feel like before we build out more teams. In October 2026 it got a deep revamp, phone first, in the direction you set: **fun, cartoon, comical, with a clean screen and comic-book pop-ups**. That's what's here:

- **Two teams, one heavily detailed backyard.** The Maple Street Mudcats visit the Cedar Lane Comets at **Pool Party Paradise**, the Mendozas' place: a two-story house behind home plate with a patio and string lights, where Mr. Mendoza (Hawaiian shirt, walrus mustache, spatula) works the grill, watches the big plays and fishes Splash Doubles out of the pool with his skimmer; an in-ground pool in shallow right; a suspiciously short picket fence; a hedge in left; trees, flower beds, flamingos, a gnome; each team's dugout (a bench, a picnic blanket and a hand-painted sign). Around it is a whole neighborhood on a bright cartoon afternoon.
- **18 cartoon kids, each one a character.** Big friendly eyes, round heads, rosy cheeks, cartoon shading and a thin ink outline. Every kid has their own face (eyes, brows, nose, smile, freckles, a gap tooth...) to fit their personality, and expressions that act like a cartoon: eyes pop wide in surprise, squeeze shut in a laugh. They move like themselves too: their own batting stance, walk and habits (Toby blows his whistle, Wren checks her binoculars, Priya taps her calculator, Jun pitches to an invisible TV camera), their own home-run trot and celebration, and they mope after a strikeout. The benches cheer, groan and high-five. Small cartoon touches (stars after a bobble, a sweat drop on a pitcher in a jam) are used sparingly.
- **Seven traits per kid: Contact, Power, Speed, Fielding, Arm, Pitching, Control**, different for everyone. **Everyone can pitch**, and a tired pitcher swaps out for a fresh arm. Nine per team, no benches, no injuries.
- **Made for thumbs.** Batting: tap the right side (or the big SWING) as the ball arrives; drag the left thumb to steer (on Rookie it aims for you); after every swing you see how you timed it ("CRUSHED / On time", "Swung under / Late"). Pitching: pick a pitch, place it, then a quick meter (tap in the green; kids with better Control get a wider green). Fielding: a four-base throw-to diamond. Running: big GO! and BACK buttons. Contact hits hard: a beat of freeze, a camera kick, slow motion on a crushed ball, a buzz on Android. A first-game coach explains each control once (replay it from How to Play). The game pauses itself when you switch apps and keeps the screen awake while you play. Mouse and keyboard still work on a computer.
- **A clean screen with comic-book pop-ups.** During play you see a small score strip, the buttons for what you're doing, and pause. The batter's trading card slides in and leaves; Chet and Dottie talk in a one-line "Channel 4½" caption. Big moments get a comic pop-up in our own words (THWACK!, SEE YA!, SIT DOWN!, WHIFF!, SNAG!, SPLOOSH!, BONK!, DOUBLE PLAY!...), each with its own sound, never the same word twice in a row; routine pitches get none.
- **Sound by the kids' garage band.** All code-made: kazoo, ukulele, toy keyboard, glockenspiel. Real backyard sounds (a wood bat crack with body, glove pops, the pool, the picket fence, Mr. Mendoza's grill, a sprinkler, cicadas, an ice-cream truck two streets over), a crowd of kids and neighbors sized to the moment, cartoon accents (a slide whistle on sky-high pop-ups, a boing off the fence), and a gibberish voice for every kid and both announcers. The commentary never repeats a line within a game. Hear everything at https://osgtax.github.io/edgefinder/#sounds.
- **Cartoon menus.** Comic lettering and code-drawn icons (no emoji anywhere): a title sign over a live CPU game with Chet doing on-air bits, team pennants, Meet the Kids as trading cards you can flip, How to Play, and Settings.
- **Fast on any device.** Every device starts on the middle graphics level and moves up or down by itself based on how smoothly it's actually running, and remembers that for next time. Far-away kids use lighter models. A browser that isn't using the graphics card gets a tip on how to turn it on. Settings has a battery saver (30 frames a second) and a speed readout.

**How we know it works:** automated tests play dozens of complete games at superhuman speed and check the stats look like a real kids' league (about a .335 team batting average, around four runs and a home run per team per game, strikeouts around 11%). Others play as a pretend human to keep hitting learnable, check the pitch meter and swing timing, check every kid has a distinct face, render every sound to make sure none clicks, clips or goes silent on a phone speaker, and play whole games to make sure the commentary never repeats.

**What changed since the v0.1 prototype:** the flat look is gone, replaced by real 3D and now a cartoon style. The pick-up draft and season mode were removed, as you asked. Seasons come back once there are 10 teams. The other 54 kids and 7 yards from the prototype are saved in the project history (commit `a00d3e8`) for when we build out the league.

## How it's built (in plain terms)

- **One game, three places.** The game is a web app, so it runs in any modern browser. For the app stores it gets wrapped in a native shell (a free tool called Capacitor) that turns the same game into an iPhone app and an Android app. One codebase, so fixes and new features land everywhere at once.
- **3D in the browser** uses Three.js, a widely used free 3D engine. The baseball simulation underneath is our own and runs separately from the graphics, which is why the automated tests can play thousands of games without drawing anything.
- **No ongoing costs to run it.** It's single-player with no servers. Settings stay on your device. Hosting the web version is free or nearly free.
- **App store costs:** Apple charges $99/year for a developer account, Google $25 once.

## Decisions for you

None of these block work. I'll keep going with the defaults in **bold** unless you say otherwise.

1. **Name.** "Grass Stain League" is a working title. (Avoid "Backyard Baseball" and "Sandlot": both are someone else's trademark.)
2. **Who it's for.** **All ages, kid-friendly** (no ads, no chat, no purchases that target kids), or aimed at adults who grew up with the originals?
3. **How it makes money (if at all).** **Pay once, around $2.99–$4.99, no ads**, or free with a one-time unlock.
4. **Order of launch.** **Web first, then Android, then iPhone.**
5. **iPhone silent switch.** **The game is silent when the phone is on silent**, like most games. It could play anyway, but that would stop any music you have playing.

## Staying original (important)

We're making a game *inspired by* the feel of 90s backyard baseball games, not a copy. Every character, name, team, yard, logo, song and joke here is new. We don't use any names, characters, art or music from those games, and no real MLB teams or players. That keeps us clear for the app stores.
