# Grass Stain League — Game Plan

*Working title. The repo will be renamed later; the name lives in one place in the code (`src/ui/app.ts`, `GAME_TITLE`).*

## The idea

A neighborhood baseball league played in people's backyards, in the spirit of the backyard baseball games we grew up with, but entirely original. Eight teams of kids play a mini "major league" season. Each team has its own yard, and every yard has its own fences, obstacles and silly ground rules.

The twist that makes it ours: **every kid is a mini adult.** They're 8 to 12 years old, but they dress, talk and play like very specific grown-ups:

- a CPA with reading glasses on a chain ("That run is non-deductible.")
- a retired plumber with a Sharpie mustache who complains about his knees
- a bingo grandma with curlers under her cap
- a pro wrestler, a used-car salesman, a lifeguard, a judge with a toy gavel, a TV weather lady, a spooky butler

Even the announcers are kids playing at being a network broadcast booth. Chet Valentine (age 10, clip-on tie) does play-by-play, and Dottie Fairweather (age 12, "former big leaguer" from one season of tee-ball) does color.

**Everything is made in code.** There are no drawings, photos, sound files or music files anywhere in the project. Every kid, yard, logo, sound effect and song is generated by the program. That keeps the game tiny (about 85 KB to download, smaller than one photo) and means any piece of it can be changed by editing code.

## Where it stands today (v0.1, a playable prototype)

**Play it:** the latest build is published as a private page on your Claude account: https://claude.ai/artifact/KGG4KBQdjTg3MHKAyHEP1U. It works on a phone (turn it sideways) or a computer.

**What's in it:**

- **Real baseball under the hood.** Balls fly with real physics: gravity, air drag, bounces that behave differently on grass, dirt, sand and mud, and caroms off fences, trees, sheds and a station wagon. Pitches curve, sink and dance. The rules are real: force outs, tag outs, tagging up on fly balls, double plays, fair and foul, walks and hit-by-pitches.
- **Kid-level play.** Kids misjudge fly balls, bobble grounders, throw rainbows from the outfield and get greedy on the bases. A typical game has a few errors and a home run or two.
- **72 kids, 8 teams, 8 backyards.** Every kid has their own look, stats, personality, bio, catchphrases and a signature special move.
- **Specials and the Hype meter.** Big plays fill your team's Hype meter. When it's full, your batter can try a Moonshot or Laser Beam, or your pitcher can throw a Brain Freeze (the ball stalls in mid-air) or a Wobbler.
- **Backyard ground rules.** Into the pool is a Splash Double. Over the barn roof is a Barn Burner home run. Hit the doghouse and the dog gets involved. Grown-ups appear in the background: Grandma Bea in her rocker, Mr. Mendoza at the grill, Grandpa Lou refusing to move his tractor.
- **Controls for phone, mouse and keyboard.** Batting: aim the sweet spot and time the swing (Power and Bunt options). Pitching: pick a pitch and tap where to throw it. Fielding: tap a base to throw there. Running: send runners or call them back.
- **Three ways to play.**
  - **Play Ball!** A quick game: pick two teams, innings and difficulty.
  - **Pick-Up Game.** Twenty kids show up at the park, and you and a CPU captain take turns picking teams.
  - **Season.** 7, 14 or 21 games, standings in two divisions, league leaders, and a four-team playoff for the Lemonade Cup. Saves automatically.
- **Three difficulty levels.** On Rookie the bat aims itself and you only need timing; All-Star gives no help.
- **Sound.** Bat cracks, mitt pops, kid crowds cheering and groaning, and four original songs, all synthesized live by the program. There's an optional announcer voice using the device's built-in speech.

**How we know it works:** automated tests play hundreds of complete games at superhuman speed and check that the stats look like a real kids' league. Right now that's about a .350 batting average, 4–5 runs per team per 6 innings, a home run or so a game and a few errors. Another test plays as a pretend human with realistic reaction times, to make sure hitting is learnable and that difficulty actually changes things.

## How it's built (in plain terms)

- **One game, three places.** The game is a web app, so it runs in any browser. For the app stores it gets wrapped in a native shell (a free tool called Capacitor) that turns the same game into an iPhone app and an Android app. One codebase, so fixes and new features land everywhere at once.
- **No ongoing costs to run it.** It's a single-player game with no servers. Saves stay on your device. Hosting the web version is free or nearly free.
- **App store costs:** Apple charges $99/year for a developer account, Google $25 once. (It looks like you may already have these from another app; if so, nothing new to pay.)

## Roadmap

The phases are in order, but they're flexible. Each one ends with something you can play.

### Phase 1 — Playable prototype ✅ *(done)*

Everything listed above.

### Phase 2 — Make it feel great *(next)*

This is the most important phase, and it needs you: play it, on your phone and your computer, and tell me what feels off. Things I already plan to polish:

- Tune the controls on real phones (swing timing, aiming feel, button sizes).
- Juicier animation: slides into bases, celebrations, kids tripping over things, the dog actually running off with the ball.
- A short first-time tutorial ("Here's how to swing").
- More commentary lines and reactions so it doesn't repeat.
- Smarter CPU managers: pitcher fatigue, relievers, intentional walks.
- A left-handed layout option, and a check of colors and contrast for color-blind players.

### Phase 3 — More game

- **Your lineup, your way:** set the batting order and positions.
- **Seasons that matter:** awards (MVP, Golden Glove), kids who improve over a season, records.
- **More of everything:** new yards (the beach house, the snowy rink yard, the neighbor's construction site), more kids, secret unlockables (Coach Dad? The ice cream truck driver?).
- **Make-your-own kid:** choose the grown-up they're imitating, their mustache, their props.
- **Two players on one device:** pass-and-play against a friend or a cousin.

### Phase 4 — Put it on the web

- Host the game at a real web address (Vercel or GitHub Pages, both free). It can be "installed" from the browser to a phone's home screen and work offline.

### Phase 5 — Ship to the App Store and Google Play

- Wrap it with Capacitor, and generate app icons and splash screens from code.
- Store listings: screenshots (captured automatically from the game), description and a privacy policy.
- Kids'-app rules: if we list it for kids, Apple's Kids category and Google's Families program require no third-party ads or tracking. The game already has none, which makes this easier.
- Beta test through TestFlight (Apple) and internal testing (Google), then release.

### Phase 6 — Optional online features

- Leaderboards, online play, cloud saves. These are the only features that add ongoing cost and complexity (servers, accounts, moderation). I'd only do them if the game finds an audience.

## Decisions for you

None of these block work. I'll keep going with the defaults in **bold** unless you say otherwise.

1. **Name.** "Grass Stain League" is a working title. Want to brainstorm others? (Avoid "Backyard Baseball" and "Sandlot": both are someone else's trademark.)
2. **Who it's for.** **All ages, kid-friendly** (no ads, no chat, no purchases that target kids), or aimed at adults who grew up with the originals?
3. **How it makes money (if at all).** **Pay once, around $2.99–$4.99, no ads**, or free with a one-time "unlock the full season" purchase. I'd avoid ads; they're a poor fit for a kids' game and create privacy headaches.
4. **Order of launch.** **Web first, then Android, then iPhone.** Web costs nothing and gets it into people's hands fastest. Android review is quick. Apple review is the strictest, so it goes last, once things are polished.
5. **The mini-adult humor.** Keep pushing it (more personas, more jokes in the commentary), or dial it back?

## Staying original (important)

We're making a game *inspired by* the feel of 90s backyard baseball games, not a copy. Every character, name, team, yard, logo, song and joke here is new. We don't use any names, characters, art or music from those games, and no real MLB teams or players (those need licenses). That keeps us clear for the app stores.
